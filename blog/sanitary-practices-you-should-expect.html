Sanitary Practices
You Should Expect.

By Flow Inc Ink Studio · 5 min read

Choosing where to get tattooed or pierced is not just an aesthetic decision — it is a health decision. Not every studio operates to the same standards, and some cut corners that directly put clients at risk. This guide tells you exactly what you are entitled to expect, so you can walk in informed and walk out safe.

Single-Use Needles: Non-Negotiable

Every reputable studio uses single-use, sterile needles that are opened in front of the client and disposed of in a sharps container after each procedure. There is no acceptable reason for a needle to be reused. Blood-borne pathogens including HIV and hepatitis B and C can be transmitted through contaminated needles. If you do not see the artist open a fresh, sealed needle package for your session — ask. If they cannot produce one, leave.

Autoclave Sterilisation for Reusable Equipment

Any equipment that is not single-use — such as grip tubes, certain piercing tools and jewellery — must be sterilised in an autoclave: a medical-grade pressure and steam machine that kills bacteria, viruses and spores. A UV light cabinet is not an autoclave and is not sufficient for sterilisation. Ask whether the studio uses an autoclave and whether it is regularly tested with spore tests.

Cross-Contamination Controls

Ink bottles should never be touched or contaminated during a session. Artists must dispense ink into individual single-use caps before beginning — dipping a needle back into a shared bottle is a contamination risk. Look out for:

  • Individual ink caps poured before the session begins.
  • Gloves worn throughout, replaced if torn or contaminated.
  • Plastic wrap or barrier film on equipment, chairs and surfaces that clients contact.
  • Surfaces that are wiped down between each client with a medical-grade disinfectant.

Clean Environment Standards

The studio environment itself signals a lot. A professional space should feel clinical — not necessarily cold, but visibly clean:

  • Floors and surfaces are free of debris and biological waste.
  • Sharps containers are present and not overflowing.
  • The artist washes or sanitises hands before gloving up.
  • Unused consumables are stored sealed and away from contamination.
  • There is no strong smell of chemical cover-up — a clean studio should not need to mask odours.

Jewellery Quality for Piercings

New piercings should only be done with implant-grade jewellery: ASTM F136 titanium, implant-grade steel (ASTM F138), solid 14k or 18k gold, or niobium. Cheaper alternatives can leach metals into healing tissue and cause allergic reactions or chronic inflammation. Ask about the specific material before confirming your jewellery selection.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Artist does not wear gloves or removes them mid-procedure without replacing them.
  • Needles are not opened from sealed packaging in front of you.
  • The studio is visibly dirty or cluttered.
  • The artist cannot answer basic questions about sterilisation.
  • Jewellery appears to be fashion-grade or the metal is not disclosed.
  • Pricing seems too good to be true — corners are being cut somewhere.
