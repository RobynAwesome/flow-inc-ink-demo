Why Sobriety
Matters.

By Flow Inc Ink Studio · 5 min read

We get it — getting tattooed or pierced can feel like a big deal, and for some people, having a drink beforehand feels like it takes the edge off. But here is the truth: arriving intoxicated is one of the fastest ways to ruin your session, your result and potentially your health. This article is not a lecture — it is a heads-up from people who genuinely care about your outcome.

Alcohol Thins Your Blood

This is the most immediate physical risk. Alcohol is a blood thinner. When you are tattooed or pierced while intoxicated, your blood does not clot at the rate it should. This means:

  • Excessive bleeding during the procedure, making it harder for the artist to work cleanly.
  • Ink spreading through wet tissue rather than sitting where it was placed — causing blurry lines and colour bleed.
  • Slower wound closure and a higher risk of infection during healing.

The result? A tattoo that heals poorly and looks different from what was designed. A piercing that takes longer to settle, if it settles at all.

Pain Perception Changes — Not in a Good Way

Alcohol does not reliably reduce pain — it alters your perception of it in unpredictable ways. Some people become more sensitive, others less coordinated. Either way, squirming or flinching during a tattoo session affects precision. Artists need a still, cooperative client to deliver clean work. An unpredictable client puts both parties at risk.

Consent Requires Clarity

Tattooing and piercing are permanent or semi-permanent procedures. Informed consent requires a clear head. A reputable studio will not perform a procedure on a visibly intoxicated client — not because they are being difficult, but because:

  • You cannot meaningfully consent to something permanent if you are not sober.
  • Studios are legally and ethically exposed if they proceed under those circumstances.
  • Regret is far more common when decisions are made under the influence.

At Flow Inc Ink, we will always decline a session if a client appears intoxicated. It protects you as much as it protects us.

The Same Applies to Certain Medications

Alcohol is the most common culprit, but several over-the-counter and prescription medications also affect bleeding and healing. These include:

  • Aspirin and ibuprofen (taken in the 24 hours before a session).
  • Blood thinners such as warfarin or rivaroxaban.
  • Certain antidepressants that affect platelet function.

Always let your artist know what medications you are taking. A good studio will appreciate the transparency. If there is any concern, a consultation before booking can help you plan the right timing.

What to Do Instead

Managing nerves before a session is completely normal. Here are approaches that actually help:

  • Eat a full meal one to two hours before your appointment — low blood sugar increases sensitivity and lightheadedness.
  • Stay hydrated. Hydrated skin responds better to needles and heals faster.
  • Bring music or a podcast. Having something to focus on is one of the most effective ways to manage discomfort.
  • Talk to your artist. Any professional worth their rate will walk you through the process and answer your questions before starting.
  • Book a consultation first if it is your first tattoo or a large piece. Familiarity with the studio and artist reduces anxiety significantly.

We Cool, But We Care and Are Compliant

Flow Inc Ink operates in a culture that celebrates self-expression, community and good energy. That vibe does not require substances — and it absolutely does not extend to letting someone damage their health in our studio. We will always respect you enough to send you home to sober up and come back another day. That is what care looks like in this environment.
